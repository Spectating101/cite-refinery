# Broad Opportunity Atlas — mapping before targeting

## What is mapped and what remains unproven

This is a **source-bounded landscape**, not a revenue opportunity leaderboard. It inventories software-capability leads across independent fields; identifies plausible *mechanism bridges*; and tracks which evidence layers are still missing.

GitHub topical search covered 44 domain strata and retained 1287 distinct public repository discovery leads. These entries are **not** evaluated product candidates or commercial targets.

An AI metadata audit labeled 220 sampled repo/domain combinations; the rest remain unreviewed. Even a 'relevant' metadata label is **not a tested capability**.

**Missing:** systematic problem evidence outside the existing limited case studies, actual tender/buyer validation, source-code functional checks, legal clearance, and conversion margins. No sector is selected.

## Reuse and conversion routes (no target selected)

| Route | Economic hypothesis | Evidence needed / stopping rule |
|---|---|---|
| self deployed service (self) | License-cleared third-party software packaged into hosted/operated service | Operational testing; explicit software rights; supported deployments; security/privacy; identifiable paying customer; stop if: No rights or no customer evidence |
| maintenance contract (for_owner) | Scoped bug repair, dependency upkeep, reliability or production hardening | Maintainer agreement; reproducible issue; task scope; budget/payout; acceptance criteria; stop if: No approval or no buyer |
| integration services (both) | Combine interoperable OSS capabilities into useful complete system | Bounded interoperability proof; operator workflow; data rights; performance targets; stop if: No operational need or mismatched interfaces |
| localization (both) | Local language, institutional, regulatory and payment workflows | Documented local requirement; legal scope; source license; deployment support budget; stop if: Localization effort exceeds addressable need |
| field data service (self) | Audited collection, validation, analysis and longitudinal monitoring | Sampling plan; consent/privacy; outcome definitions; buyer with ongoing need; stop if: Measurements cannot resolve decision |
| open source bounties (for_owner) | Solve published bounty-backed GitHub issue | Verified funded current issue; permission to claim; accepted payout conditions; stop if: No active bounty or payout process |
| public procurement (both) | Respond to specific published funded RFP | Live notice; eligibility; procurement rights; delivery capacity; contract economics; stop if: Cannot bid or serve required location |
| retrofit repair (self) | Diagnostic-repair-resale or contract refurbishment | Parts availability; failure rates; safety; warranty; local supply/demand margin; stop if: Expected net profit nonpositive |
| open hardware products (self) | Assemble and validate licensed open hardware designs | BOM; build instructions; safety approval; inventory; tested unit economics; stop if: Production safety or cost unknown |
| maintainer partnership (for_owner) | Convert under-supported upstream project into operated product or service | Mutual agreement; brand/IP/data rights; ownership of support and revenue; buyer demand; stop if: Maintainer does not consent |
| auditable evidence (both) | Sell verification of implementations, contracts or claimed outcomes | Independently checkable facts; sector process; buyer that values reduced uncertainty; stop if: No independent information advantage |
| grant delivery (both) | Develop open-source public good against explicit grant milestones | Call eligibility; project outputs; grant terms; sustainability beyond award; stop if: No eligible call or unfunded continuation |

## Critical semantic and market blind spots

A keyword/topic match is often false: flood can mean a DDoS attack; repair can mean JSON correction; battery can mean a desktop status utility. The mapping pipeline tracks and labels these rather than silently asserting technical fit.

The public repositories are just a biased first-page cross-section of GitHub's tagged projects: other forges, industrial repositories, project websites, non-English documentation, hardware prototypes, institutional implementations, and low-profile systems need further sampling.

The next **mapping** pass, before any target selection, should add real practical incident/cost evidence, official procurement requirements, maintainer funding/constraints, cross-forge software inventory, and semantic source-level review. Project or field selection remains separate.

## Safety and inference boundaries

- Repository metadata and topic labels are untrusted navigation candidates, not source-reviewed software capabilities.
- Semantic auditing of descriptions does not establish code quality or real deployment.
- Cross-domain mechanism links are explicit hypotheses, not independently demonstrated equivalences.
- Published procurement metadata does not establish an open, funded-for-us, bidder-eligible or awarded contract.
- A registered demand/procurement channel does not establish a currently open, funded, eligible contract.
- Popularity, low stars and issue counts are not economic underconversion or paying demand.
- The frozen PR #70 research corpus and its private holdout are not merged or changed.
- No targets are selected or scored; a later evaluation requires buyer/rights/technical evidence.
