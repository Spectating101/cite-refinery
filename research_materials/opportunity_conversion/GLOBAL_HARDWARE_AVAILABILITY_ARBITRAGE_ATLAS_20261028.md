# Cross-market technology and hardware arbitrage atlas: broad-scope execution frame
**Research freeze: 2026-10-28 | Owner ecosystem: Cite–Refinery discovery → HS engineering and commercial conversion**

## Correct scope
Do **not** optimize the portfolio around one robotic pet, or even around robots. The starting unit of analysis is the **customer need × exact product function × geography × supply channel × technical alternative × full delivered economics**. Continuously inspect widely different physical product families, including ordinary products that are unusually costly, poorly serviced or hard to acquire in a particular country.

**Core hypothesis:** some products and practical engineering methods already exist globally, but a given Indonesian or Taiwanese buyer experiences poor availability, expensive delivered prices, inadequate local adaptation, bad warranties/repair, insufficient interfaces, missing accessories, high integrator labor, or simply a missing adjacent *function*. HS could make economic sense through sourcing, OEM/ODM, integrating existing modules, adapting, manufacturing, leasing, repairing, or operating assets rather than automatically rebuilding the original OEM design.

**Current evidence level:** broad discovery + individual published seller examples; **ZERO validated supply gaps, purchase commitments, qualified manufacturing costings or unit profits**. Absence from one marketplace or an expensive premium reference price cannot prove unmet demand.

## Classify arbitrage mechanisms before selecting a build
1. **Import/distribution:** product genuinely unavailable or slower/more costly locally; confirm exact model supply and true landed price, registration, warranty and customer support.
2. **Price-to-performance redesign:** premium competitor anchors feature expectations; build lower-cost functionally comparable alternative, potentially trading away features deliberately.
3. **Localization:** language, connectivity, payment, local electrical standards, spare parts, durability, installation and user workflows.
4. **Capability recombination:** reuse open-source robotics, PCBs, firmware, vision and mature subsystems into a functional configuration no convenient product offers.
5. **Industrial service arbitrage:** reuse HS to identify model/variant, documentation, test plan, qualified repair supplier, replacement routes and cost.
6. **OEM/ODM/private label:** adopt existing reliable product/hardware with negotiated batch terms; do not reinvent commodity motors, batteries or boards.
7. **Refurbish/upcycle/exchange:** condition, repair yield, responsible data handling, proof of physical QA, returns, exact sale comps.
8. **Operate/rent/managed equipment:** the customer buys a function (inspection, monitoring, training, exhibit) while the operator retains and maintains equipment.
9. **Underserved accessories & interoperability:** replacement parts, protocols, connectivity bridge, reusable mounts, calibration/maintenance kits.
10. **Knowledge-to-market matching:** publish a qualified specification and send market-verified sourcing/engineering opportunities to partners.

**Novelty is not required**. Strong competitors can confirm use cases and reveal overpriced bundles. Still test hidden costs: R&D, maintenance, software subscriptions, cloud, model usage, repair returns, logistics, safety, certification, warranty, IP/license and distribution, which may make a high sticker price rational.

## Evidence ladder and scoring
A candidate becomes economically rankable **only after**:
1. Exact consumer/SME/job and recurring frequency or actual buying problem.
2. Comparable **global** actual product/feature reference, with variant and ownership/license.
3. **At least two independent local sellers/substitutes searched**, respecting duplicates and missing sources; a negative claim of shortage needs deeper query/search and buyer confirmation.
4. Actual verified or documented quoted sourcing route and fully burdened acquisition.
5. Technical route with conditional cost, physical maturity, realistic yield and quality gate.
6. Legal permission/import/classification/wireless and electrical product standards check.
7. Expected cash contribution *per lead/unit*, and comparison to the best off-the-shelf substitute.

Until then: scores are **null/unknown**, not fabricated 8/10 'arbitrage potential'. The first discovery queue may use **research convenience** only (public buyer/seller accessibility and nonhazardous testability), and must not be presented as an investment ranking.

**Unit economics by route:**
- Resell: cash collected − all-in landed cost − platform fees − marketing − returns/warranty − work/overheads − finance/capital costs.
- OEM/assemble: per-unit sell-through adjusted receipts − prototype amortization − qualified BOM + labor + test + tooling + regulatory burden + distribution + returns, accounting for failed builds.
- Service: collected contracted fees − qualified technician / lab / subcontractor + report + acquisition + unsuccessful intake + warranty and dispute.
- Operate: actual measured client output/value minus full O&M, compliance, capital and downtime cost; do not call hypothetical productivity 'cash revenue'.
- Subscription/add-on: actual active paying users minus recurring infrastructure and field support.
Use sensitivity analysis for supply success, demand conversion, warranty rate, exchange rate, taxes and tariffs; never flatten these into a fictional universal margin.

## First execution: market diversity before feature depth
**Wave 0: broad survey.** Audit the 64 hypotheses as a **discovery inventory**. Research at least two providers or substitutes and one buyer demand channel in both Indonesia and Taiwan for a selected subset. Add date, URL, exact variant, SKU, seller identity and actual stock/price meaning. Eliminate clear copycat/no-demand ideas early.

**Wave 1: stratified 12-case validation** (not one robot):
- 2 social/pet/consumer smart products
- 2 STEM/education products
- 2 farm/aquaculture sensing/control products
- 2 industrial MRO or shop test/inspection products
- 2 energy/environment/monitoring products
- 2 cold-chain/facility/retail operation products

For each: exact product reference, existing local choices, required functions, estimated landed cost range only if quote backed, safety/compliance gates, sourcing alternative, HS build-or-integrate benchmark, and 1 buyer signal. **No need to purchase any item merely to screen it.**

**Wave 2: select 3 pathways**, not 3 arbitrary products: (a) resell/OEM sourced, (b) HS integrated or modified, (c) B2B service/lease. Each must beat a real established substitute under equivalent functional requirements.

**Wave 3: physical and paying validations** only after legal/qualified capacity and permission: exact BOM, independently measured function, actual warranty and return plan, buyer prepayment/contract or settled sale. No unsupervised flight, robotics motion, hazardous power, plant safety, or medical claims.

### Stop/hold decisions
Reject or hold for absent repeatable demand, suspicious single-source seller claims, missing comparable SKU, higher fully burdened cost than equivalent incumbent, denied access to licensed software, no safety/compliance route, low repeat frequency relative to service burden, or evidence that HS cannot physically meet performance requirements. A startup market with no competition may mean no buyer.

**Prioritize creating a broad comparison and execution engine**, not selecting one showcase robot.
